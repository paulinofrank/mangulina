## Research findings and limits

- [El Día (2023)](https://eldia.com.do/johnny-tulanga-un-compositor-y-musico-olvidado/) is based partly on Tulanga's testimony and documents La Romana/San Pedro beginnings, Los Hijos del Rey, studio work, compositions and specific arrangements. Its clause about «La Ola» blurs the orchestra and vocalist; a [copyright-registration transcription](https://www.copyrightencyclopedia.com/el-derecho-y-el-deber-%3D-the-lawful-and-the-obligation-and-1-2/) names «Juan Luis y orquesta» as performer on «Construyendo una idea» (1985), while [the archival audio caption](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x4ifuaz) identifies Juanchy Vásquez as vocalist. The proposed text accommodates both roles without treating them as competing composers.
- The same registration transcription identifies Tulanga as the author of both «La Ola» (registration PA0000272650) and «Cuentos de terror» (PA0000248363), and lists `1955-` after his name. This is a secondary presentation of copyright records. Before importing composition credits or setting a birth year, inspect the underlying registration. The public biography currently describes only the «Cuentos de terror» arrangement corroborated by El Día.
- [Fausto Polanco's 2026 article on Joe Nicolás](https://eldia.com.do/joe-nicolas-el-bajista-que-llevo-a-ramon-orlando-a-los-pies-de-cristo/) dates Tulanga's departure from Ramón Orlando's orchestra to late 1986. The article does not establish why Tulanga left, and the proposed text makes no causal claim.
- [La Romana's November 2020 municipal bulletin](https://alcaldialaromana.gob.do/transparencia/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/BOLETIN-ALCALD%C3%8DA-NOVIEMBRE-word.pdf) documents recognition of Tulanga for his musical contributions. Its wording oddly names a separate «Juan de la Cruz» alongside «Johnny Tulanga»; the March 2021 bulletin, which explicitly pairs his stage name with his full personal name, is used to resolve the identity. The March bulletin also records municipal medication support, which is retained as research evidence but omitted from the public biography as unnecessary personal detail.
- The two user-supplied sheets point to 2026 Instagram and Facebook reels praising Tulanga; those reels were not independently retrievable in this review, so no ranking or "first bassist" assertion is proposed. A single video biography reportedly gives 29 March 1955, while an age stated in a 2024 interview implies a different year. Birth date and year remain blank pending stronger reconciliation.
- The sheets' account of imprisonment and claims that he was the first to bring specific techniques to merengue remain uncorroborated or contested. They are omitted. No Spotify or official social identity is verified; those fields stay empty. No releases, song credits, media, or other artists' biographies change beyond the two exact Los Hijos del Rey reference repairs above.
