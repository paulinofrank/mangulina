## Evidence and decisions

1. **Early life.** Santana says in the Silvio Mora interview, part 1, and the Cabrera transcript (3:06–8:55) that he was born in El Estrecho de Luperón, grew up in Vuelta Larga, learned guitar there, worked from 1977 in the Natalio Redondo dairy operation at Sabaneta de Yásica and later Luperón, and moved to Santo Domingo around 1981. The birthplace is already corrected in the structured artist field. The proposed biography addition is attributed to his testimony; it does not fabricate a diploma or conservatory training.
2. **Newspaper job.** In Silvio Mora's 2022 interview (11:12–16:18) and TrayectoriaRD's 2026 transcript (about 10:01–11:18), he recounts work as a messenger at *El Nuevo Diario* and publication of an early article with editor Juan Bolívar Díaz's permission. This is relevant to his writing formation and may be added as a first-person recollection.
3. **«Y te vi» and the tax.** The current biography says its release *coincided* with the creation of “ITBIS”. In the Cabrera interview (about 18:25–19:02), Santana explicitly says the lyric was a veiled criticism of the new transfer tax. A later [commentary article](https://www.porlalinea.com.do/el-proyecto-de-modernizacion-fiscal-un-parche-mas-al-sistema-tributario/) independently recognizes the song's tax allusion. More importantly, the Dominican tax authority [dates the introduction of **ITBI** to 1983](https://dgii.gov.do/publicacionesOficiales/estudios/Documents/2022/Nota%20Tecnica%202022-02%20-%20An%C3%A1lisis%20sobre%20la%20Productividad%20y%20Eficiencia%20del%20ITBIS%20en%20Republica%20Dominicana.pdf) and says it did not acquire the **ITBIS** name until 2000. A [2012 Koki Acosta interview](https://testigouno.blogspot.com/2012/06/koqui-acosta-cuando-surgi-me-criticaron.html) identifies the song as Santana's. Public copy should state the original tax name and attribute his account of intent.
4. **Mozaribe.** The current biography omits Santana's own group. The [J&N Publishing acquisition announcement](https://internewsservice.com/2022/02/09/eeuu-j-n-publishing-confirma-adquisicion-de-catalogo-de-mas-de-140-obras-del-compositor-dominicano-frantoni-santana/) confirms that he recorded Mozaribe in the 1990s, using strings in merengue and writing most of its songs. The [Amazon Music listing for *Apurando el Paso*](https://music.amazon.in/albums/B0CQ3TT4TZ) gives a May 14, 1996 edition date; a derivative reproduction of a U.S. copyright entry gives 1992 as the creation date for a possibly differently titled Mozaribe recording. Do not assert a precise debut or recording year from these alone. No Mozaribe artist record was found in Mangulina, so no artistReference can yet be added.
5. **Publishing.** The same 2022 J&N announcement documents acquisition of more than 140 Santana compositions. This is a dated, checkable career milestone distinct from WIPO's estimate of more than 250 recorded works; the figures measure different things and should not be reconciled by arithmetic.
6. **Birthday.** Two Facebook congratulations on October 10 in 2012 and 2013 support the day and month. Derivative reproductions of U.S. copyright records variously attach **1956** and **1957** to Francisco Ramón Santana; the difference is unresolved, and the original registrations have not been inspected. No year or full DOB is proposed. The artist's `date_of_birth` stays null.
7. **Disputed credits and allegations.** Interviews contain one-sided accounts of who originated «La faldita», production of «El baile del perrito», alleged interference with Mozaribe, and disagreements with other musicians. These are excluded from the biography and other profiles. Credits for songs must follow the discography evidence and review gates, not transcript assertions alone.

### Exact proposed English prose

Replace the first paragraph beneath “Early career and breakthrough in merengue” with:

> In Vuelta Larga, Imbert, Santana learned his first guitar chords and sang with a small local group. He began working for the Natalio Redondo dairy company in Sabaneta de Yásica in 1977 and later worked in Luperón.
